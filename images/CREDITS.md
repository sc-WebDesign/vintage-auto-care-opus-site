## The two that were replaced, and why

**Both hotlinked images failed inspection, and neither problem was in the
description attached to them.**

- The one captioned "timing belt" was **not a timing belt**. It is an accessory
  drive, on the outside of the engine, with the air-conditioning compressor
  visible. A garage's own website calling that a timing belt is the sort of
  error a mechanic spots in a second, on a page whose whole argument is
  competence. It also carried a legible **KWIK PERFORMANCE** badge.
- The atmospheric hands-and-spanner shot is a **classic car** engine: the
  radiator core and carburettor linkage give it away. On a business named
  *Vintage* Auto Care that is actively misleading, because the name is a brand
  rather than a description and he works on everyday modern cars.

## Others rejected

- A gloved hand with a battery tester: **VW roundel** on the engine cover and
  **alfatest** on the tool.
- Two frames of a diagnostic tablet reading **MaxiSys**, with the screen in
  Spanish saying *Licencia caducada*, an expired software licence.
- A workshop with a car on a lift: **SKODA** badge, **MIRKA** and **WURTH**
  equipment, and a spray booth, which is body and paint work rather than
  mechanical repair.
- An old workshop carrying a **Ford Granada** poster and a **Motorcraft** sign.
- A large multi-bay workshop with several cars in progress. No brand problem,
  but it shows a scale of operation that contradicts the whole point of the
  page: one man, and the same one every visit.
